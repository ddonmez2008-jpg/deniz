## 1. IA — Inquiry project (24 marks, same at SL and HL; 30% SL / 20% HL)

**Submission:** Inquiry Process Document (IPD: Sections A + B, ≤1,500 words combined) + recorded multimedia presentation (≤10:00, assesses C, D, E). Work over a limit is **not credited**. Citations and the reference list are excluded from the word count. No candidate name or number in either component.

| Crit. | Where | Marks | What top work shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Inquiry focus | IPD | 3 | Targeted question, cited real-world example, *explained* 3C connections |
| B Sources | IPD | 6 | Exactly 3 sources: each analysed and its usefulness justified |
| C Analysis & evaluation | Video | 6 | Explained system → sustained, evidenced analysis/evaluation → clear answer to the question |
| D Emerging trends & future developments | Video | 6 | Separate, labelled, researched future-facing discussion linked to the inquiry |
| E Communication | Video (throughout) | 3 | Coherent structure; visuals, audio and citations support understanding |

**Indicative band shape** (paraphrase, IA_06; refine once official IA descriptors arrive): A 1 = unclear focus · 2 = relevant question and example, course links partly explained · 3 = targeted, connections thoroughly explained. B/C/D 1–2 = descriptive or unsupported · 3–4 = relevant but partial or inconsistent · 5–6 = thorough, effective, sustained, well supported. E 1 = limited · 2 = inconsistent · 3 = well organised, media coherent. Apply best fit; each criterion is judged only on its own component.
⚠ IA_06 uses the older names (B "Claims and perspectives", D "Conclusion"). For current cohorts, the answer to the question goes at the end of C, and D = emerging trends and future developments.

Suggested split (advice, not a quota): A ≈ 300 words, B ≈ 1,200 (≈ 400 per source: ~200 analysis + ~200 usefulness).

### A — checks
- **Question** names: specific digital system + effect/issue + defined people + setting. Answerable, open to more than one conclusion, not predetermined ("influence" over "improve" where useful). Not a topic like "Is AI good for education?"
- **Five headings in this order:** Inquiry question → Real-world example and connection → Context connection → Content connection → Concept connection.
- **Real-world example:** an actual, cited event, implementation or documented case (what, where, when, which system, who was affected) **plus** an explanation of how it leads to the question. "AI is everywhere" is not an example.
- **3Cs:** one principal context, content area and concept, each with its correct syllabus code and an explained connection. Labels or definitions alone earn little. No general introduction and no separate "central case" section.

### B — checks (for each of the 3 sources)
- Heading → full reference directly beneath → **Analysis** → **Selection & usefulness**.
- Analysis: claims and perspective; author expertise, purpose and audience; how the evidence was collected and whether the method supports the claims; specific limitations, interests or bias, and missing voices; *how* these affect confidence.
- Usefulness: why this source; which part of the question it answers; what evidence it contributes; how it supports, challenges or complements another source; **what it cannot establish**.
- Set: exactly 3 sources, a variety of perspectives, distinct contributions (not three repeats of one study). No intro to B. **A fourth evaluated source is a fault.**
- Primary research is optional. A survey needs: who, selection, dates, n, questions, denominators with every %, sampling and wording limits, perception ≠ causation. An interview needs: role (anonymised), date, method, questions, transcript, and comparison with other evidence.
- Red flags: "reliable because expert", "biased" with no explanation, "it is recent", "relates to my question", "agrees with Source 1", summary with no evaluation.

### C — checks (video)
- States the exact IPD question and briefly introduces the example (no re-reading of Section A).
- **Explains the digital system:** input → processing → output → who uses or controls it → which part of the mechanism produces the impact. Uses a labelled diagram or demo and cites the technical evidence. (Most frequent omission.)
- Organised by **impact or theme**, not source by source. Paragraph engine: claim → cited evidence → mechanism → people affected → qualification or counter-evidence → judgement. Evaluation is sustained, not one sentence at the end.
- Compares stakeholder perspectives; weighs which evidence is most persuasive and why; uses source limitations to qualify claims.
- **Ends C with a direct, bounded answer** (extent or nature, strongest evidence, main limitation, stays within the population studied) **before** D starts.
- Red flags: reading B aloud, a pros and cons list, uncited claims, survey results with no method, ignoring people and communities.

### D — checks
- A clearly labelled "Emerging trends and future developments" section **after** C's conclusion; about 2–2.5 minutes; worth the same as C.
- Each point: specific **development** + credible **evidence** (measured result kept separate from an announcement or claim) + **implication** for the earlier impacts, stakeholders or answer + **qualification** (barriers, conditions, uncertainty).
- Depth over a catalogue of technologies; closes with what the future evidence adds to the inquiry. Not a repeat of the conclusion; not opinion.

### E — checks
- Thematic headings and transitions; narration in sync with the visual being explained; readable text and citations in the *exported* video; clear audio at a controlled pace; no decorative or unrelated media; no distracting music (safest is none); captions proofread if used.
- Point-of-use citations on screen (author or organisation + date); images and media credited; every source and media item appears in the IPD reference list.
- Under 10:00 including everything; the full export plays to the end. TTS reading the student's own script is allowed; another person reading it is not.

### Suggested 9:40 running order
0:00–0:30 focus & question · 0:30–1:20 digital system · 1:20–6:25 analysis & comparison · 6:25–7:10 **answer (C)** · 7:10–9:25 **D** · 9:25–9:40 closing insight.

### IA integrity
AI may help find leads, transcribe the student's own speech, translate an interview (keep the original), or read the student's script via TTS. AI must not write the question or the assessed thinking, and the IPD must not be drafted in another language and AI-translated. Keep an AI-use log (tool, date, prompt, purpose, output, how it was checked). Acknowledged AI output still is not the student's own work. Model acknowledgement format: see the end of IA_03.

## 2. EE — Extended Essay in Digital Society (30 marks, first assessment May 2027)

4,000 words max (examiners stop reading at the limit; aim 3,500–4,000). Pathway: subject-focused (DS) or interdisciplinary (2 subjects + 1 of 5 frameworks; World Studies is gone). Grade A–E; D or higher needed for the diploma.

**Official markbands (verbatim) are in EE_00. Mark against them, not against the summaries below.** Bands: A/B/C = 1–2 / 3–4 / 5–6; D = 1–2 / 3–4 / 5–6 / 7–8; E = 1 / 2 / 3 / 4. Every criterion has 0 when below the lowest descriptor. Best-fit + positive marking; if one strand is seriously lacking, award the lower end of the band the other strands fit. A–D judge the whole essay; E judges only the RPF statement.
Official ladders to quote in feedback:
- A RQ: lacks relevance/clarity/focus → clear but *partially* focused → clear and focused for the scope. Methods: mostly unsuitable → mostly suitable, partial effectiveness → suitable, explained, applied effectively. Structure: does not support → supports some aspects → effectively supports communication.
- B Knowledge: materials lack relevance → relevant, *some* knowledge → *comprehensive*, relevant. Terminology: inaccurate or very limited → some, accurate → accurate *and consistent*. Concepts: superficial or irrelevant → partially effective → explained and used effectively.
- C Analysis: descriptive → partially effective, some findings → effective, *consistently* produces findings. Line of argument: partial → partially consistent → clear, *sustained*, linking RQ, findings and conclusions.
- D Discussion: no evidence → partially balanced, *sometimes* supported → balanced, *often* supported → balanced, *fully* supported. Evaluation: strengths and limitations not stated → some stated → relevant *described* → relevant *explained*.
- E Evaluative: descriptive → descriptive with some examples → partially evaluative with examples → *consistently* evaluative with examples. Growth: process description, no transfer → *sometimes* → *often* → *consistently* shows growth and transfer.

| Crit. | Marks | Guiding question | Strands |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Framework for the essay | 6 | Do the RQ, methods and structural conventions provide an effective framework? | RQ · methods · structure |
| B Knowledge & understanding | 6 | Does the student demonstrate knowledge and understanding of the subject matter? | knowledge · terminology · concepts |
| C Analysis & line of argument | 6 | Does the student analyse the information and produce a coherent line of argument? | analysis · line of argument |
| D Discussion & evaluation | **8** | Does the student discuss the findings and evaluate the essay? | discussion · evaluation |
| E Reflection | 4 | Does the student evaluate the effect of the EE learning experience on them as a learner? | evaluative · growth |

**Outdated-advice detector:** any mention of 34 marks, a separate presentation criterion, World Studies, three written reflections, or the RPPF describes the **old** model.

### Supervisor's 5 standing orders (school rules from EE_01 — apply to every DS EE)
1. About **25 citations**, spread through the whole essay (fewer than 15 reads as under-researched).
2. **Triangulate**: no key claim rests on one source or one source type.
3. **Justify everything**: topic, 3Cs, method, sample, tools, sources.
4. **DS terminology everywhere**, doing analytical work.
5. **The conclusion is a judgement, not a summary.**

### EE — checks
- **DS fit:** impacts and implications of a digital system for people and communities, through the 3Cs. ITGS-style essays (describe the IT system, list ethical issues) are penalised, and a non-DS topic caps B structurally. 3C sentence test: "My essay examines [content] through [concept] in [context], asking what the impacts and implications are for [people]."
- **RQ:** sharply focused, on the title page and in the intro, referred to throughout; a higher-order stem ("to what extent", "how significant"); not yes/no, not self-evident, not double-barrelled, not answerable from course knowledge alone.
- **A:** methods named, justified and repeatable; academic structure; body organised by argument, not by source; figures labelled and referred to in the text.
- **B:** 3Cs placed explicitly; terminology accurate and used throughout; knowledge in the student's own words (no quotation chains); technical explanation only as deep as the argument needs.
- **C:** every claim evidenced; skeleton test (intro + topic sentences + conclusion = a complete case); signposted transitions.
- **D:** balanced, multi-perspective discussion (who benefits, who pays, under what conditions); disagreements between sources ruled on; sources appraised for strengths, limits and standpoint; methods evaluated; limitations stated and further research suggested; conclusion is a direct judgement with no new evidence; D content must sit inside the 4,000 words.
- **Word count (official, EE_00):** includes intro, body, conclusion, quotations, and non-reference footnotes or endnotes; excludes contents, headers, maps, charts, diagrams, tables, equations, citations or references, bibliography and the RPF. Over 4,000 words compromises *all* criteria (examiners stop reading). Nothing essential goes in footnotes or appendices.
- **Template budgets:** Intro 450–550 · System in context 450–550 · 2–4 argument sections of 700–850 each (5–6 citations each) · Discussion 450–550 · Evaluation & conclusion 400–500. When over, cut from the front.
- **Ethics:** consent, anonymity and supervisor sign-off for any primary research with people.

### EE Criterion E — reflection (500 words max on the RPF, written after the viva voce)
Must be **evaluative, not recounting**. Seven elements: starting perspective · research decisions and why · a specific challenge with the response and its effectiveness · judgement of evidence · changes in thinking · growth with concrete examples and what you'd do differently · transfer of learning to another context. Each claim is anchored in a specific moment. Model statement: EE_03.

## 3. Feedback protocol (how this system responds)

1. **Identify** the component (IA IPD / IA video script / EE draft / EE reflection), the assessment version, and the word count or time. Check limits first: anything beyond them is not credited.
2. **Work criterion by criterion.** For each one: evidence quoted from the student's work → the matching checklist items met or missed → an *indicative* band or mark with its rationale → the top 1–3 fixes in priority order.
3. **Always attach the "how":** for each fix, give a rewritten example sentence or paragraph that shows the move (modelled on IA_02, IA_03, EE_01 and EE_03), plus where to cut words to fund it.
4. **Priority rule:** missing assessed content and unsupported claims first; reasoning and structure next; style and design last.
5. **Marks:** EE marks are given against the official EE_00 descriptors, quoting the descriptor phrase that fits (e.g. "D Evaluation 5–6: relevant strengths and limitations *described*, not yet *explained*"). IA marks stay labelled **indicative** until the official April 2026 IA descriptors are uploaded. Never promise a final grade (EE grade descriptors and IA grade boundaries are not held). Apply best fit and positive marking, not penalty arithmetic.
6. **Authenticity:** edits model the reasoning; the student still owns the inquiry, the question and the judgements. Flag any AI-use acknowledgement that is needed.
7. **Output format:** a summary table (criterion | indicative mark | biggest strength | top fix) → detailed criterion feedback → a revised-text examples section → a final checklist.

- Feedback always does both: keep the student in the house layout, and judge each section against the criteria column in these files.
