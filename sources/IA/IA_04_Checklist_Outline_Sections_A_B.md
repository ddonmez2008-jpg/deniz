Digital Society IA checklist and outline
Sections A and B
Combined word limit: 1,500 words. Around 300 words for A and 1,200 for B is a suggested allocation, not two
compulsory quotas.

Section A Inquiry focus
Use these five headings, in this order.

1 Inquiry question
☐ State the exact inquiry question clearly.
☐ Identify a specific digital system, the effect or issue being investigated, and the people and setting.
☐ Keep the question focused, answerable through available evidence and open to different conclusions.

2 Real world example and connection
☐ Describe an actual event, implementation or documented case directly.
☐ Explain what happened, where and when, which digital system was involved, and who was affected.
☐ Include relevant, verified details rather than a general introduction to the topic.
☐ Cite the example where it appears.
☐ Explain how the example leads to the inquiry question.

3 Context connection
☐ Name the principal Digital Society context and give its correct syllabus code.
☐ Explain how this context connects the example, affected people and inquiry question.

4 Content connection
☐ Name the principal digital content area and give its correct syllabus code.
☐ Explain the relevant technology or mechanism and its connection to the issue.

5 Concept connection
☐ Name the principal concept and give its correct syllabus code.
☐ Explain how this concept helps investigate the example and question.
Section A should contain the question, real-world example and explained connections. Avoid an additional
general introduction or a separate “central case” discussion.

Section B Sources
Source selection checklist
☐ Select exactly three sources for detailed evaluation.
☐ Choose sources that offer a variety of perspectives, such as those of affected people, practitioners,
 developers, institutions or independent researchers.
☐ Ensure the sources make distinct contributions and allow comparison or challenge.
☐ Avoid selecting three reports that simply repeat the same original study or announcement.
☐ Select sources because of their relevance and evidence, rather than prestige or agreement with a
 preferred answer.
Sources can include research papers, reports and other relevant published material, as well as primary
research such as surveys or interviews. There is no compulsory primary/secondary mix. Primary research is
optional.

Repeat this outline for each source
Source 1 — Title or clear description
Source 2 — Title or clear description
Source 3 — Title or clear description
Under each source heading, include the following.

Full reference
☐ Place the complete reference directly beneath the source heading.
☐ Use one consistent referencing style and provide sufficient details to locate the source.

Analysis
☐ Identify the relevant claims, findings and perspective.
☐ Explain the author’s or organisation’s relevant expertise, purpose and intended audience.
☐ Examine how the evidence was collected and whether the method supports the claims.
☐ Identify specific limitations, possible interests or bias, and missing perspectives.
☐ Explain how these strengths and limitations affect confidence in the evidence.

Selection and usefulness
☐ Explain why this particular source was selected.
☐ Identify the part of the inquiry question it helps answer.
☐ Specify the evidence, explanation or perspective it contributes.
☐ Explain how it supports, challenges or complements another source.
☐ State what it cannot establish and how this limits its use.
About 200 words of analysis and 200 words on selection and usefulness per source is a planning guide.
Prioritise purposeful evaluation within the combined word limit.

Section B Primary research sources
If a selected source is a survey
☐ Explain who was approached, how participants were selected, when it ran and how many responded.
☐ Identify the relevant questions and findings; give denominators alongside percentages.
☐ Discuss sampling bias, question wording and limits on generalisation.
☐ Distinguish reported perceptions or associations from evidence of causation.

If a selected source is an interview
☐ Identify the interviewee’s relevant role or expertise, using appropriate anonymity.
☐ Record the date, method, relevant questions and responses used.
☐ Explain the value and limitations of this person’s perspective.
☐ Compare their account with other evidence rather than treating one interview as representative.

Final checks
☐ A and B are clearly separated and remain within 1,500 words combined.
☐ Section B evaluates exactly three sources and includes both required parts for each.
☐ Important factual claims have citations at the point of use.
☐ A complete reference list follows B and covers sources used in both the IPD and presentation, including
 relevant media and acknowledged tools.
☐ Supporting questionnaires, transcripts or anonymised research records are included where useful;
 essential methods and evaluation remain in the main work.
