Digital Society IA video checklist and outline
Criteria C D and E
Keep all assessed content within 10 minutes. Use the research to build an argument about people and
communities; do not read Sections A and B aloud. Criterion E applies throughout the recording.

Criterion C Analysis and evaluation
1 Inquiry focus
☐ State the exact inquiry question used in the IPD.
☐ Briefly introduce the actual example, digital system, affected group and setting.
☐ Make the issue being investigated clear without repeating all of Section A.

2 How the digital system works
☐ Identify the specific system or application and the relevant inputs or data.
☐ Explain how the system processes those inputs and what output or action results.
☐ Explain who uses the output and who controls relevant settings or decisions.
☐ Use a labelled diagram, interface or demonstration to connect the mechanism to the impacts; cite the
 technical evidence.

3 Analysis of impacts and implications
☐ Organise the discussion around relevant impacts or themes, rather than one section per source.
☐ For each main claim, provide cited evidence and explain how the digital system produces an effect for
 identified people.
☐ Compare a variety of stakeholder perspectives and explain why experiences or interests differ.
☐ Weigh contrasting findings, benefits, harms, trade-offs and conditions; explain which evidence is most
 persuasive and why.
☐ Use source limitations to qualify the impact claims, including uncertainty about causation or
 generalisation.
☐ Reach reasoned judgements throughout, then combine the findings into an answer; avoid a descriptive
 list of advantages and disadvantages.

4 Answer to the inquiry question
☐ Give a clear answer stating the extent or nature of the effect, supported by the strongest evidence.
☐ Weigh the main counter-evidence or limitation and keep the answer within the population and setting
 studied.
☐ Complete this conclusion within Criterion C, before introducing emerging trends and future
 developments.

Criterion D Emerging trends and future developments
Begin this clearly labelled section after the conclusion of C. Allow roughly 2–2.5 minutes as a planning
recommendation. Develop further insight into the same inquiry rather than repeating the earlier conclusion.

5 Identify a relevant development
☐ Identify a specific developing practice, technical change, trial, adoption pattern or other relevant
 development.
☐ Show how it connects to the digital system, people and issue already investigated.
☐ Select developments that can be explored in depth; avoid a catalogue of unrelated technologies.

6 Establish the research evidence
☐ Cite credible evidence that the development exists or that supports the forecast.
☐ Distinguish a measured result from a proposal, announcement, marketing claim or aspiration.
☐ Explain relevant limits of the evidence, such as a small pilot, different population or preprint status.

7 Explain implications and uncertainty
☐ Explain how the development could change the earlier impacts, stakeholder relationships or answer to
 the question.
☐ Explain the mechanism behind that possible change rather than stating that technology will improve
 everything.
☐ Discuss relevant barriers, conditions and alternative outcomes; make forecasts conditional where the
 evidence requires it.

8 Closing insight
☐ End with what the future-facing evidence adds to understanding the original inquiry.
☐ Explain what evidence or conditions would strengthen or change the judgement.
☐ Keep the final insight inside the assessed time limit.

Suggested running order
The following is an illustrative plan, not compulsory section timings or a minimum recording length.
00:00–01:20 Inquiry question, example and digital system
01:20–06:25 Impacts, contrasting perspectives, comparison and synthesis
06:25–07:10 Answer to the inquiry question within C
07:10–09:25 Emerging trends and future developments
09:25–09:40 Closing insight, leaving a small timing buffer

Criterion E Communication throughout
Structure and multimedia
☐ Use clear thematic headings and transitions so the audience can follow the argument.
☐ Synchronise narration and visuals; explain the relevant part of each chart, diagram, image or
 demonstration.
☐ Keep text, labels, units, citations and evidence readable in the exported recording.
☐ Use intelligible audio and a controlled speaking pace; remove distracting visuals or sound.
☐ If captions are used, check them against the speech. Captions and an appendix script are recommended,
 not compulsory.

Evidence and references
☐ Identify the author or organisation and date when research is shown or discussed; use a readable
 on-screen or clear spoken citation.
☐ Credit images, charts, clips, audio and other media as well as factual claims; identify original material
 appropriately.
☐ Use additional relevant research where needed; the presentation is not limited to the three sources
 evaluated in B.
☐ Ensure every presentation source and relevant media item appears in the complete reference list
 accompanying the IPD.
☐ If using survey findings, state who responded, recruitment or selection, response count, method and
 main limitation; give denominators with percentages.
☐ If using an interview, establish the interviewee’s relevant role, date, method and perspective before
 relying on their responses.

Final export and submission checks
☐ Time the complete recording, including clips, transitions, pauses and closing material; content after 10:00
 is not credited.
☐ Watch the entire final export and check audio, visuals, captions if used, citations and the ending.
☐ Confirm that the C conclusion appears before a clearly labelled D section and that neither is rushed or cut
 off.
☐ Check the school’s current file-format, anonymity, acknowledgement and submission instructions.
☐ Open or preview the submitted copy where possible and verify that the correct complete recording was
 uploaded.
There is no compulsory slide count or application. A shorter recording has no automatic penalty, but it must
still contain sufficiently developed analysis and future-facing discussion.
