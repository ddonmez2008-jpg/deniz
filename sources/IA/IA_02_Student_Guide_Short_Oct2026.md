DIGITAL SOCIETY / IA STUDENT GUIDE  1/5

Your IA: the format + your focus
A practical student guide • 24 marks • Five pages

  What you submit  What goes inside

  Inquiry Process Document  A: Inquiry focus - 3 marks (about 300 words).
  1,500 words maximum for A + B  B: Three sources - 6 marks (about 1,200 words).
  Then the full reference list and useful appendices.

  Recorded multimedia presentation  C: Analysis and evaluation - 6 marks.
  10 minutes maximum  D: Trends and future developments - 6 marks.
  E: Communication - 3 marks.

The word split is advice; the total limits are firm. Work beyond 1,500 words or 10 minutes is not credited. Citations
and the reference list are excluded from the word count.

A | Start with a question you can actually answer
Too broad: “Is AI good for education?”
Focused: “To what extent does Tool X’s AI feedback influence how Year 12 English students
revise their writing at School X?”
Why it works: one named tool, one effect, one group and one setting. Replace Tool X with the actual system you
investigate.

  In Section A  What strong writing does

  Real-world example  Identify a specific, documented use of the tool. Cite it and explain how it
  leads to your question. “AI is everywhere” is not a real-world example.

  Context: human  “This inquiry examines how students develop writing skills through
  knowledge  feedback.” → Shows the learning context.

  Content: artificial  “It investigates how AI-generated suggestions influence students’
  intelligence  revision decisions.” → Connects the technology to the issue.

  Concept: power  “It considers whether students retain control over their writing or defer to
  the tool’s suggestions.” → Gives a lens for analysis.

  Explain the connections
  Develop one main context, content area and concept clearly. Do not spend your words listing
  several labels or copying definitions.

About the samples: Tool X, School X and all findings in this guide are fictional teaching examples. Bracketed
citations are placeholders. Use your own verified evidence and real references. Confirm the applicable assessment
version with your teacher.

B | Analyse and justify three sources
6 marks • Exactly three evaluated sources in Section B

For each source: source heading → full reference → analysis → usefulness. Aim for about 400
words each: roughly 200 for analysis and 200 for usefulness. These are flexible targets.

  ANNOTATED SAMPLE • SOURCE ANALYSIS

  [1] The provider’s report claims that Tool X helps students revise independently [Provider,
  year]. [2] Its technical explanation is useful for understanding how feedback is produced. [3]
  However, the provider benefits from sales, and the report does not explain how users were
  selected. [4] Its positive results therefore need comparison with independent research.

  Annotation  Why it helps

  [1] Claim and perspective  Identifies what this source argues and whose view it represents.

  [2] Specific value  Explains what the source can help establish.

  [3]-[4] Limitation and  Explains the weakness and what it means for trusting the claim.
  judgement

Then explain why you selected it
  SAMPLE • USEFULNESS TO THIS INQUIRY

  “I will use this report to explain the tool’s intended benefits. I will compare these claims with
  independent research and students’ accounts of revision. This will help me judge the gap
  between the intended use and actual practice. The report alone cannot show that students at
  School X learn more.”

Why this works: it gives the source a clear job, plans a comparison and limits the conclusion. “It is reliable and
relevant” does none of these.

Build a research base you can defend
Use more than the three B sources where relevant in the presentation. More references alone
do not earn marks: use their evidence.
Primary research is optional. If you use it, explain the method briefly in the presentation and
provide the fuller evidence in an appendix.

Interview: relevant role, date, method, questions and transcript.
Survey: who responded, how they were selected, dates, questions, number of responses, results
and limitations. Never give “75%” without the sample size.

  Avoid these mark-losing habits
  Summarising without evaluating; calling a source “biased” without explaining why; forgetting
  usefulness; adding a fourth source to B; treating a small survey as proof about everyone.

C | Explain, analyse, evaluate
6 marks • Your conclusion answering the question belongs here

First explain the system: what goes in → what the technology does → what comes out. For Tool
X, explain how submitted writing becomes feedback. Verify the actual tool’s mechanism and cite
it.
Then build an argument: claim → evidence → explanation → qualification → judgement. Repeat
this reasoning through the presentation. Do not just list advantages and disadvantages.

  ANNOTATED SAMPLE • ANALYSIS AND EVALUATION

  [1] Tool X appears to help students begin revising sooner. [2] In the school survey, 18 of 24
  respondents said they revised before the next lesson [Survey, date]. [3] Immediate feedback
  may explain this: students can act without waiting for a teacher’s next comments. [4]
  However, one teacher reported that some students accepted suggestions they could not
  explain [Interview, date]. [5] Faster revision therefore does not necessarily mean deeper
  learning. The survey also measures students’ reports, not improvement in writing quality.

  Sentence  What you are doing

  [1] Claim  State an impact that answers part of the inquiry question.

  [2] Evidence  Support it with a traceable finding and a clear sample size.

  [3] Analysis  Explain how a feature of the system may produce the effect.

  [4] Comparison  Introduce evidence that qualifies the first finding.

  [5] Evaluation  Judge what the evidence proves and what it does not.

Finish C with a direct answer
  SAMPLE • CONCLUSION OF THE ANALYSIS

  “Overall, Tool X appears to influence when these students revise more clearly than how
  deeply they learn. The survey suggests earlier revision, but the interview raises doubts about
  understanding. My evidence supports a change in reported revision habits; it does not
  establish improved writing quality.”

Why this works: it answers the question, combines evidence and sets limits. It does not claim a universal result.

  What drops marks?
  Describing sources instead of analysing impacts; unsupported claims; ignoring people and
  communities; repeating B aloud; saving all evaluation for the last sentence.

D | Show what may change next
6 marks • Research this section; do not guess

After the conclusion of C, show the heading “Emerging trends and future developments”.
Explain a developing change, its evidence and what it could mean for your inquiry. Allow about
2-2.5 minutes.
Weak: “AI will become better and all students will benefit.”
Problem: no specific change, no evidence, no conditions.

  ANNOTATED SAMPLE • A RESEARCHED FUTURE INSIGHT

  [1] A pilot report describes a feedback tool that asks students to explain a correction before
  accepting it [Pilot report, year]. [2] This could shift revision from accepting answers to
  considering reasons. [3] For the students in my inquiry, that could address the concern about
  following suggestions without understanding them. [4] However, the pilot covers only one
  class, so wider benefits remain uncertain.

  Annotation  What earns its place

  [1] Development + evidence  Names a specific change and cites support.

  [2] Implication  Explains how the change could affect behaviour.

  [3] Link to your inquiry  Connects the future discussion to the earlier finding.

  [4] Qualification  Treats a possible benefit as uncertain, not guaranteed.

A suggested 9:40 presentation plan
  Time  What to cover

  0:00-0:30  Introduce your focus and state the inquiry question.

  0:30-1:20  Explain the digital system.

  1:20-6:25  Analyse and evaluate impacts; compare evidence and perspectives.

  6:25-7:10  Conclude C: give your evidence-based answer.

  7:10-9:25  Discuss emerging trends and future developments.

  9:25-9:40  Close with the significance of that future-facing discussion.

  Protect the final section
  D is worth the same six marks as C. Do not leave it to the final few seconds or fill it with a
  repeat of your main conclusion. Keep all assessed content before 10:00.

Timings are flexible. There is no required slide count or separate quota for trends and future developments.

3 marks • Clear structure, useful visuals and understandable audio

  Do this  Avoid this

  Use clear section headings and logical transitions.  A string of disconnected images or facts.

  Show the graph, screenshot or diagram you are  Unrelated footage or decorative effects.
  explaining.

  Use clear narration; proofread any captions.  Rushed speech, distracting music or incorrect
  captions.

  Cite evidence where you use it.  Only putting a bibliography on the final slide.

A simple citation model
  ON THE SLIDE

  Finding: 18 of 24 respondents reported revising before the next lesson.
  Short citation: Student survey, [date], n = 24; see Appendix A.
  In the narration: “This is a self-selected sample from one class, so it cannot represent all
  students.”

This is fictional sample data. For a published source, use a short author/organisation citation linked to its full
reference. Credit borrowed images as well as factual claims.

Before you submit
□ A: focused question, cited real-world example and explained three-C connections.
□ B: exactly three sources, each analysed and justified.
□ C: explained technology, cited evidence, evaluation and a clear answer.
□ D: a separate, researched discussion linked to the inquiry.
□ E: relevant visuals, readable citations and clear audio.
□ Limits: A + B ≤ 1,500 words; complete recording ≤ 10 minutes.
□ References: the IPD lists all sources and media used in both components.
□ Research: necessary interview or survey evidence is included.
□ Files: the entire exported recording plays correctly, including the end.

  Keep the work yours
  Use your own reasoning and writing. Record and acknowledge any AI use, verify outputs and
  follow your teacher’s guidance. Acknowledging AI does not make generated analysis your own
  work.

Final checks: follow your school’s file-format rules. The webinars say not to put your name or candidate number
inside the IPD or video. Captions and an appendix script are optional.
