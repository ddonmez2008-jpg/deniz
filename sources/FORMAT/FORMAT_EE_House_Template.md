Opening anchor = a specific cited case or statistic, not a grand claim. |
| 3 | **System section**: how the digital system works (sample: "The Democratization of …"). | "System in context" 450–550 words: accurate technical vocabulary, only as deep as the argument needs (Crit. B). Define the scope deliberately excluded. About 4–5 citations. |

| 5 | **Mechanisms section** as a main heading with **3–4 indented sub-headings** (one mechanism each). | The core argument (Crit. C). Each sub-section: point (DS terminology) → evidence from **≥2 independent cited sources** → analysis (why it holds, what it cannot show) → link back to the RQ. Organised by argument, never by source. 700–850 words and 5–6 citations per argument section. |
| 6 | **Further thematic argument sections** (e.g. a literacy or inequality strand, a social or psychological strand). | Same paragraph anatomy. Each must add a new strand of the answer, not repeat one. Total argument sections: 2–4 major ones. |
| 7 | **Case Study** section. | Strongest when it carries evidence (data, platform reports, dated events) that *tests* the mechanisms above. Cite specific figures and dates. Anecdote or hypothetical examples ("for instance a copywriter…") are not evidence. |
| 8 | **Ethical Considerations** section. | Use DS concepts explicitly (values and ethics, power) and course terms. Ideally fold into the Discussion so that D work is visible. |
| 9 | **Counterarguments and Limitations** section. | = **Discussion** (Crit. D, 450–550 words): weigh the strongest counter-perspective with evidence, **rule on where sources disagree**, who benefits, who pays, under what conditions. "Limitations" here must evaluate **the essay's own sources and methods** (D evaluation strand: strengths and limitations *explained*), not only limits of the technology. |
| 10 | **Conclusion** (3 paragraphs). | = **Evaluation & conclusion** (Crit. D, 400–500 words): direct judgement on the RQ (to what extent, under what conditions), synthesis not summary, evaluation of the essay's effectiveness, unresolved questions, **further research**, **no new evidence**. |
| 11 | **References** (APA 7, alphabetical, DOIs or URLs). | Standing order: about **25 sources**, varied types, **every entry cited in the text and every citation listed**. Homepage-only URLs (e.g. https://www.ilo.org) are not locatable, so give the full document URL. One consistent style. |
| 12 | *(Not in sample; add when used)* Appendices. | Survey or interview instruments, consent, raw data summaries. Nothing essential to the argument goes here. |

## Skeleton to enforce
```
TITLE PAGE   Title · Research question · Subject: Digital society [+ 2nd subject + framework] · Word count   (no identifiers)
CONTENTS     headings + sub-headings + page numbers (match the body exactly)
1  Introduction                      450–550   anchor · significance · RQ verbatim · 3Cs justified · methodology justified · thesis · roadmap
2  The system in context             450–550   how it works (accurate terms) · context and scope · ~4–5 citations
3–5 Argument sections (2–4)          700–850 each   claim-led headings · optional indented sub-headings · 5–6 citations each, triangulated
   [Case study — inside or as its own argument section, evidence-led]
6  Discussion (counterarguments)     450–550   weigh perspectives · rule on disagreements · who benefits/pays/when
7  Evaluation & conclusion           400–500   judgement · essay strengths + limitations explained · open questions · further research · no new evidence
References   ~25, consistent style, two-way matched
Appendices   instruments only
```
Total of about 3,600–4,000 words. When over the limit, cut from sections 1–2 first, never from 6–7.

## Known failure patterns seen in the sample format (check every student for these)
1. **No in-text citations in the body.** The essay reads as opinion, which hits B (knowledge), C (analysis), and D ("supported by appropriate evidence"). This is the single most damaging pattern.
2. Theorists named (e.g. a theory or author) but not cited or referenced in the text.
3. No methodology paragraph (Crit. A methods strand) and no explicit 3C framing (Crit. B concepts strand).
4. A "Limitations" section about the technology rather than the essay's own research (D evaluation strand).
5. Conclusion that summarises ("this can be done through…") rather than judging and evaluating.
6. Pathway mismatch: "interdisciplinary approach" claimed in a subject-focused essay.
7. ToC or heading errors (duplicated or garbled heading text). Speculative claims ("will", "will lose access") without evidence.
8. Word count well under 4,000 (sample 3,611): room left for D-criterion work.
