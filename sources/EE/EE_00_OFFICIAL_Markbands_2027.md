## How marks are applied (pp. 111–112)
- Criteria **A, B, C, D** are applied to the essay **as a whole**. Criterion **E** applies **only to the reflective statement on the RPF**.
- **Best-fit + positive marking.** Examiners read from the highest markband down to the descriptor that most accurately describes the work. They give credit for what is there and do not hunt for omissions.
- It is not necessary for every indicator in a band to be met. If two of three strands fit a band well but one is seriously lacking, award that band at the **lower end**.
- Work that falls between two bands: reread both and choose the better fit. Use the upper marks in a band when the qualities are shown to a greater extent, the lower marks when they are shown to a lesser extent.
- "The highest level descriptors do not imply faultless performance and should be achievable by a student."
- Every criterion: if the work does not reach the lowest descriptor, it earns **0**.

## Criterion A — Framework for the essay (6)
**Guiding question:** Do the research question, research methods and structural conventions followed provide an effective framework for the essay?

| Strand | 1–2 | 3–4 | 5–6 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Research question | A research question is stated but it lacks relevance to the topic of investigation, clarity or focus. | The research question is relevant to the topic of their investigation and clear but only partially focused in relation to the scope of the essay. | The research question is relevant to the topic of investigation, clear and focused in relation to the scope of the essay. |
| Research methods | Research methods are used, but these are mostly unsuitable for the research question. | Research methods that are mostly suitable for the research question are explained and applied with partial effectiveness. | Research methods that are suitable for the research question are explained and applied effectively. |
| Structure | Structural conventions are present in the essay but these do not support communication of the research. | Structural conventions used in the essay support some aspects of the communication of the research. | Structural conventions used in the essay effectively support communication of the research. |

## Criterion B — Knowledge and understanding (6)
**Guiding question:** Does the student demonstrate knowledge and understanding of the subject matter being used in their research?

| Strand | 1–2 | 3–4 | 5–6 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Knowledge | Research materials are used to establish knowledge of the subject matter but these materials lack relevance. | Relevant research materials are used to establish some knowledge of the subject matter. | Comprehensive, relevant research materials are used to establish knowledge of the subject matter. |
| Understanding — Terminology | Terminology is used but it lacks accuracy or is very limited. | Some relevant terminology is used accurately to demonstrate understanding of the subject matter. | Relevant terminology is used accurately and consistently to demonstrate understanding of the subject matter. |
| Understanding — Concepts | Concepts are identified but these are not relevant or are explained superficially. | Some relevant concepts are explained and used with partial effectiveness to demonstrate understanding of the subject matter. | Relevant concepts are explained and used effectively to demonstrate understanding of the subject matter. |

## Criterion C — Analysis and line of argument (6)
**Guiding question:** Does the student analyse the information presented in the essay and produce a coherent line of argument?

| Strand | 1–2 | 3–4 | 5–6 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Analysis | The essay is descriptive rather than analytical. | The essay includes analysis that is partially effective and produces some relevant findings. | Analysis in the essay is effective and consistently produces relevant findings. |
| Line of argument | A partial line of argument is present. | A partially consistent line of argument links the research question, research findings and conclusions. | A clear, sustained line of argument links the research question, research findings and conclusions. |

## Criterion D — Discussion and evaluation (8)
**Guiding question:** Does the student discuss the findings and evaluate the essay?

| Strand | 1–2 | 3–4 | 5–6 | 7–8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Discussion | The significance of the findings is described, but there is no reference to supporting evidence. | A partially balanced discussion of the significance of the findings is sometimes supported by appropriate evidence. | A balanced discussion of the significance of the findings is often supported by appropriate evidence. | A balanced discussion of the significance of the findings is fully supported by appropriate evidence. |
| Evaluation | A partial evaluation of the effectiveness of the essay is present, but strengths and limitations are not stated. | An evaluation of the effectiveness of the essay is present, with some strengths and limitations stated. | An evaluation of the effectiveness of the essay is present, with relevant strengths and limitations described. | An evaluation of the effectiveness of the essay is present, with relevant strengths and limitations explained. |

Key verb ladder for D evaluation: *not stated → some stated → relevant described → relevant **explained***.

## Criterion E — Reflection (4)
**Guiding question:** Does the student evaluate the effect of the extended essay learning experience on them as a learner?
Applies **only** to the reflective statement on the RPF (500 words max, written at the end of the process, after the viva voce).
Evidence may be drawn from, but is not limited to:
- experiences and insights that could shape future thinking
- explicit examples of how skills learned could be used in other current contexts and in the future
- how changes in perspective impacted decision-making.

| Strand | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Evaluative | Reflection on the EE learning experience is descriptive. | …is descriptive and includes some specific examples. | …is partially evaluative and includes specific examples. | …is consistently evaluative and includes specific examples. |
| Growth | Reflection on the student's growth is limited to a description of the process and there is no evidence of transfer of learning. | Reflection sometimes shows evidence of the student's growth and transfer of learning. | Reflection often shows evidence of the student's growth and transfer of learning. | Reflection consistently shows evidence of the student's growth and transfer of learning. |

## Alignment with assessment objectives (pp. 114–115)
- A → Knowledge & understanding (relevant RQ, knowledge of methods) + Communication of research (structural conventions)
- B → Knowledge & understanding (research materials; terminology and concepts)
- C → Application & analysis (analysis gives relevant findings) + Synthesis & evaluation (line of argument links RQ, findings, conclusions)
- D → Synthesis & evaluation (balanced discussion of significance; effectiveness of essay evaluated)
- E → Synthesis & evaluation (learning experience evaluated; growth reflected on)

## Word count rules (pp. 87–90) — official
- Upper limit **4,000 words**. Examiners are instructed **not to read or assess** material beyond the limit, so an over-length essay is "negatively compromised across all assessment criteria".
- **Included:** introduction, main body, conclusion, quotations, footnotes or endnotes **that are not references**.
- **Not included:** contents page, headers, maps, charts, diagrams, annotated illustrations, tables, equations, formulas and calculations, citations or references (parenthetical, numbered, footnotes or endnotes), bibliography, the RPF. Acknowledgements and dedication pages do not count (and must contain no identifiers).
- Anything important to the line of argument must **not** be placed in appendices, footnotes or endnotes, because examiners are not required to read them. Using notes to evade the limit compromises performance across the criteria.
- Non-English sources: quote in translation in the body, with the original in a footnote (that footnote is not counted).

## Individuals & societies (Group 3) guidance applied to Digital Society (pp. 55–63)
- All EEs require secondary research (literature review). Primary research is allowed in DS as appropriate. *(Unlike psychology, which is secondary only.)*
- **A:** RQ and methods must clearly align with the DS lens (check the subject guide). Structural conventions: label diagrams, maps (scale, key), images and graphs; acknowledge others' work; refer to them actively in the text; use headings appropriately.
- **B:** use subject terminology, e.g. "digital systems" in digital society. Apply key concepts meaningfully and explanatorily.
- **C:** "own" the theories, concepts and methods, and weave findings toward conclusions that answer the RQ.
- **D:** balanced discussion considers different perspectives and stakeholders; evaluate by weighing the strengths and limitations of the essay.
- **Official DS example RQs:**
  - "How has the increase in information shared on social media challenged democratic institutions? The case of Cambridge Analytica." (secondary: documentaries, podcasts, books, news; primary: Cambridge Analytica publications, Zuckerberg's Congress testimony, Facebook ads; analysis of impacts on components of democracy)
  - "To what extent has factory work been transformed by automation in the last decade? To what extent can productive work cultures be maintained with robo-colleagues?" (case study, stakeholders, financial statements over time, employee interviews)
- **Official interdisciplinary examples involving DS:** DS + design technology (simulation game design / Culture, identity, expression); DS + biology or psychology (social media addiction / Movement, time, space); economics + DS (asymmetric information in internet scams / Power, equality, justice); DS + business (Instagram influencers / Evidence, measurement, innovation).

## Reflection process (pp. 97–100)
Three mandatory reflection sessions (first, interim, viva voce), recorded on the RPF, recommended 20–30 minutes each. Check-ins between sessions are not recorded. The supervisor reads the final version before the viva voce. A researcher's reflection space (RRS) is strongly recommended and is the main raw material for the statement.
