STANDING ORDERS
Five rules that hold for every digital society extended essay, from first proposal to final upload. Each
one exists because essays that ignore it lose marks in predictable, avoidable ways. Print this page and
keep it above your desk.

1  A I M F O R 2 5 C I TAT I O N S
  A well-researched essay cites around 25 sources, spread through the whole essay, not clustered in
  the background section. Every finding, every statistic, every claim about the world carries a citation.
  If a body page has no citations on it, that page is opinion, and examiners mark it as such. Quality
  still rules: 25 strong, varied sources beat 40 thin ones, but fewer than 15 almost always reads as
  under-researched for this subject.

2  T R I A N G U L AT E Y O U R S O U R C E S
  No important claim rests on one source or one type of source. Check each key finding against at
  least two independent perspectives: academic research against journalism, a platform's own report
  against a regulator's or a critic's, survey data against published studies. Where sources agree, your
  finding is strong; where they disagree, say so, weigh them, and explain which you trust more and
  why. That disagreement is not a problem, it is Criterion D material.

3  JUSTIFY EVERYTHING
  Every choice you made is defended in writing, in the essay itself. Why this topic and why it matters.
  Which concepts, content, and contexts frame it, and why those three. Why this methodology and not
  the alternatives. Why this sample, this size, these people. Why these tools, this survey design, these
  interview questions, these sources. An unjustified choice looks like an accident; a justified one looks
  like research. The examiner should never have to guess why you did anything.

4  D I G I TA L S O C I E T Y T E R M I N O L O G Y E V E R Y W H E R E
  Course language runs through the entire essay, not just the introduction. Concepts named and used
  as analytical tools. The system described in accurate technical vocabulary. Terms like algorithmic
  curation, datafication, platform governance, digital divide, surveillance, automation, moderation,
  doing real work in your sentences. If a paragraph could appear unchanged in a history or business
  essay, rewrite it until it could not.

5  T H E C O N C L U S I O N I S A J U D G E M E N T, N O T A S U M M A R Y
  Never end by repeating what the essay already said. The conclusion answers the research question
  directly, with a position: to what extent, how significant, under what conditions. It synthesises your
  findings into that answer, states what the evidence could not settle, and points to the research that
  should come next. If the conclusion could be written by someone who read only your section
  headings, it is a summary. Start again.

W H AT T H E E X T E N D E D E S S AY I S
The extended essay is a 4,000-word piece of independent academic research on a topic you choose
yourself. It sits in the core of the Diploma Programme alongside TOK and CAS, it is compulsory, and it
is externally assessed by IB examiners. You will spend roughly 40 hours on it across more than a year,
guided by a supervisor at your school through three mandatory reflection sessions.

Combined with your TOK grade, the essay contributes up to 3 points to your diploma total. You must
earn at least a D grade on the essay to be awarded the diploma at all. So the stakes are real, but so is the
payoff: this is the closest thing to university-level research you will do at school, and admissions tutors
know it.

 L E N GT H  4,000 words maximum. Examiners stop reading at the limit, so anything past it simply
  does not exist. Aim for 3,500 to 4,000.

 MARKS  30 marks across five criteria (A to E), converted to a letter grade from A to E.

 REFLECTION  Three reflection sessions with your supervisor. The third is a short concluding
  interview, the viva voce. Afterwards you write one reflective statement of up to 500
  words on the Reflection and Progress Form (RPF).

 TIME  About 40 hours of your work. Three to five hours of supervision.

 PA S S C O N D I T I O N  A grade of D or higher is required for the diploma.

 SUBJECT CHOICE  Either one DP subject you study (subject-focused) or two DP subjects combined
  (interdisciplinary). This guide assumes digital society is your subject or one of the
  two.

Every extended essay now travels down one of two pathways. Both are assessed against the same five
criteria, both produce a 4,000-word essay, and neither is graded more generously than the other. The
choice depends entirely on the nature of your topic.

T H E S U B J E C T- F O C U S E D P A T H W A Y
This is the classic extended essay: you use the knowledge, concepts, theories, perspectives, or methods
of one DP subject to answer a research question. For you, that subject is digital society. The IB strongly
recommends writing in a subject you actually study, and for good reason: your essay must show
disciplinary grounding, and it is very hard to fake fluency in a course you have never taken.

A subject-focused digital society essay lives inside the boundaries of the digital society course. Chapter
3 explains exactly what that means, because it is the single most common place where digital society
essays go wrong.

T H E I N T E R D I S C I P L I N A R Y PAT H W AY
New for 2027, this pathway replaces World Studies. You integrate knowledge, concepts, theories,
perspectives, or methods from two DP subjects to answer a question that neither subject could answer
well alone. Integration is the key word. Writing half an essay in one subject and half in the other does
not qualify; the two lenses must work together, and your conclusion should show the reader what the
combination made visible.

The rules worth memorising:

 • You should be studying at least one of the two subjects. Studying both is even better.
 • Environmental systems and societies and literature and performance cannot be used on this
  pathway. They are already cross-disciplinary, so they belong to the subject-focused pathway only.
 • The balance between the two subjects does not need to be even. It follows the demands of your
  question.
 • Your essay must not duplicate work you submit elsewhere in the diploma, such as an internal
  assessment.

The five frameworks
Every interdisciplinary essay is registered under one of five frameworks. The framework shapes and
positions your topic during the inquiry stage; it is not itself assessed, and your topic does not need to
touch every word in the framework's name. One framework, two subjects, one question.

W H AT M A K E S A N E S S AY D I G I TA L S O C I E T Y
Here is an uncomfortable fact from a recent examiner report: around three quarters of digital society
extended essays read like essays for ITGS, the course digital society replaced. Students analysed an IT
system, listed some ethical and social issues, and stopped. Those essays were penalised, because digital
society is a different course with a different architecture.

A digital society essay examines the impacts and implications of digital systems for people and
communities, and it does so through the course's inquiry framework, the 3Cs:

Concepts

Change  Power  Values and ethics

Expression  Space

Identity  Systems

These seven ideas are the deep questions of the course. A strong essay makes one or two of them do real
work: an essay on facial recognition in policing is an essay about power and values and ethics, and it
should say so, explicitly, in those terms.

Content

Data  Networks and the internet  Robots and autonomous tech

Algorithms  Media

Computing devices  Artificial intelligence

This is the digital system at the centre of your essay. You need to explain how it works with accurate
technical vocabulary, at the depth needed to support your argument, and no deeper. The essay is about
the system's consequences, not its engineering; an essay dominated by technical description belongs in
computer science, and examiners will say so.

Contexts

Cultural  Health  Social

Economic  Human knowledge

Environmental  Political

This is where real people and real communities enter. Anchoring your essay in a specific context, a
country, an industry, a community, a time period, is what turns a vague topic into a researchable one.

WRITING THE RESEARCH QUESTION
The topic is the territory; the research question is the exact path through it. The official guide is precise
about what your question must do. It should be sharply focused, answerable within 4,000 words, stated
early in the essay and on the title page, and open to critical examination rather than a yes or no. It
should invite analysis, evaluation and synthesis, never description. And it should not lead to a self-
evident answer: if the reader can guess your conclusion from the question alone, there is no research to
do.

Higher-order stems do most of this work for you. Questions beginning to what extent, how significant,
how successful, or how has X affected Y force judgement. Questions beginning what is or how does X work
invite a report.

 TOO BROAD, DESCRIPTIVE, OR OFF-SUBJECT  FOCUSED AND RESEARCHABLE

  assessment tools in US courts reinforced existing
  inequalities in sentencing?

  system reshaped political expression among young
  voters in Türkiye since 2022?

Two more cautions. First, avoid double-barrelled questions that bolt two inquiries together; the scope
will drown you. Second, your question must not be answerable from course knowledge alone. The essay
exists to make you research beyond the classroom.

  THE FIVE
  CRITERIA

Each criterion now opens with a guiding question. This is a gift: it is the literal question the examiner
holds in mind while reading your essay. Each criterion is also divided into strands, and examiners locate
each strand in a mark band before settling on a best-fit total. Below, each criterion is unpacked with its
strands and what it demands of a digital society essay in particular.

A  F R A M E W O R K F O R T H E E S S AY
  6 MARKS
  Do the research question, the research methods, and the structural conventions followed
  provide an effective framework for the essay?

  STRANDS: RESEARCH QUESTION · RESEARCH METHODS · STRUCTURE

  Criterion A judges the skeleton. Your research question must be focused, stated early, and
  sustained: refer back to it throughout the essay, not just in the introduction and conclusion.
  Your methods section must explain what you did and why those methods suit the question. In
  digital society this means naming your approach honestly: a comparative case study, content
  analysis of platform policies, a survey of a defined group, semi-structured interviews, analysis
  of published datasets. Explain primary methods in enough detail that someone could repeat
  them.

  Structure now lives here too, absorbed from the old presentation criterion. Follow the
  conventions of an academic essay in the humanities and social sciences: introduction, a body
  organised by argument rather than by source, conclusion, complete references. Figures and
  tables appear only when they carry weight in your argument, always labelled and always
  referred to in the text.

B  K N O W L E D G E A N D U N D E R S TA N D I N G
  6 MARKS
  Does the student demonstrate knowledge and understanding of the subject matter being
  used in their research?

  S T R A N D S : K N O W L E D G E · U N D E R S TA N D I N G : T E R M I N O L O G Y · U N D E R S TA N D I N G : C O N C E P T S

  This is where the 3Cs pay their rent. Your essay must place the research question in the
  broader context of digital society, use course terminology accurately and consistently, and put
  the course concepts to purposeful work. Writing about power asymmetries, datafication,
  algorithmic curation, or platform governance, correctly and where the argument needs them,
  signals disciplinary grounding. Sprinkling jargon over a generic essay signals the opposite, and
  examiners can tell the difference in a paragraph.

  Your knowledge should be visible in your own writing. Long quotation chains suggest you have
  collected sources rather than understood them. Explain, in your own words, and cite.

C  A N A LY S I S A N D L I N E O F A R G U M E N T
  6 MARKS
  Does the student analyze the information presented in the essay and produce a coherent line
  of argument?

  S T R A N D S : A N A LY S I S · L I N E O F A R G U M E N T

  Analysis means breaking your topic into its working parts and establishing findings from
  evidence, rather than reporting what happened. Every analytical claim needs support from your
  research. If a paragraph could survive with its evidence deleted, it is description wearing
  analysis's clothes.

  The line of argument is the new, explicitly rewarded element. It is the reasoned thread
  connecting your research question, your findings, and your conclusion, with signposts that tell
  the reader where the argument has come from and where it goes next. The connections
  between sections are your job as the author, never the reader's job to infer. A practical test:
  read only your introduction, your topic sentences, and your conclusion. If that skeleton makes
  a complete, logical case, your line of argument holds.

D  D I S C U S S I O N A N D E V A L U AT I O N
  8 M A R K S · T H E H E AV I E S T C R I T E R I O N
  Does the student discuss the findings and evaluate the essay?

  S T R A N D S : D I S C U S S I O N · E VA L U AT I O N

  Eight marks, and most of them are earned or lost in the final third of the essay. Discussion
  means a balanced review of what your findings mean: multiple perspectives, competing factors,
  and a conclusion that synthesises rather than summarises. In digital society, balance matters
  doubly, because the temptation to write a one-sided essay about technology being good or bad
  is strong, and examiners reward students who resist it. The impacts of digital systems are
  almost always uneven: who benefits, who pays, and under what conditions?

  Evaluation turns the lens on your own essay. Weigh the strengths and limitations of your
  sources and methods. Note the stated or implied positions of the writers you cite: a platform's
  transparency report, a regulator's press release, and an activist NGO's study each have a
  standpoint. Acknowledge what your evidence cannot show, and suggest where further research
  would logically go. This self-awareness is not weakness; under this mark scheme it is where the
  top band lives.

  WORD COUNT WARNING

  Discussion, evaluation, and conclusion sit at the end of the essay. If you run past 4,000
  words, these are precisely the sections the examiner never reads, and Criterion D
  collapses. Edit ruthlessly from the front of the essay, never the back.

A review of existing literature, secondary research, is required in every extended essay, whatever the
pathway. Most group 3 essays, digital society included, combine that secondary base with primary
research where it strengthens the question. Recent digital society examiner reports are blunt on both
fronts: essays need a wide variety of quality secondary sources, and any primary research needs a
thorough, explicit methodology.

Research ethics
If your research involves people, and in digital society it often does, informed consent, anonymity, and
care with sensitive data are requirements, not decorations. Surveying classmates about social media and
mental health, for example, touches wellbeing and needs your supervisor's sign-off on the instrument
before anyone answers a question. The guide devotes a whole section to the ethical researcher; read it
before collecting anything.

S T R U C T U R E T H AT S E R V E S T H E A R G U M E N T
The essay follows the standard academic shape. What matters is that each part does its one job.

 SECTION  ITS JOB

 Title page  Research question, subject (and second subject plus framework if interdisciplinary),
  word count.

 Introduction  The topic, why it matters, the research question, your methodology, and a map of
  the argument to come.

 Body  The teaching sequence of your argument: context and system explained briefly, then
  findings built section by section, each tied back to the question. Organise by
  argument, never source by source.

 SECTION  ITS JOB

 Discussion & conclusion  What the findings mean together, weighed and balanced; the answer to the
  question; limitations; where research goes next. No new evidence here.

 References  One consistent citation style, every in-text citation matched in the list and vice

Section headings are allowed and often help in group 3 essays; just make sure they mark stages of one
continuous argument rather than chopping the essay into disconnected reports.

T H E W O R D C O U N T, P R E C I S E LY
The 4,000 words include your introduction, main body, conclusion, and quotations. They exclude the
title page, contents page, labelled tables and figures, equations, citations and references, the
bibliography, and appendices. Two practical consequences follow. First, do not smuggle argument into
footnotes or appendices; examiners are not required to read them, so essential material buried there is
lost. Second, treat 4,000 as a hard ceiling and 3,500 as a healthy floor: well short of the limit usually
means the analysis is thin.

  IN PRACTICE

  Draft long, then cut from the front. Introductions and background sections carry the most fat;
  discussion and evaluation carry the most marks. A finished digital society essay usually explains
  the technology in half the space of the first draft and spends the savings on Criterion D.

ACADEMIC INTEGRITY
Every source, idea, and borrowed phrase is acknowledged, in the style your school prescribes,
consistently. This includes AI tools: if any generative tool contributed to your process in a way your
school and the IB's academic integrity policy permit, it must be acknowledged as the policy requires.
Unacknowledged use is treated as academic misconduct, and the IB investigates. When in doubt, cite,
and ask your supervisor first rather than after.

THE THREE REFLECTION SESSIONS
You will meet your supervisor for three mandatory reflection sessions across the process, with informal
check-ins in between. Keep a researcher's reflection space (RRS) from day one: a notebook, document,
or folder where you record decisions, dead ends, changes of direction, and what you learned from each.
It is not submitted, but it is the raw material your reflective statement will be built from, and honest
notes made in the moment beat memories reconstructed a year later.

 SESSION  WHEN  W H AT I T I S F O R

 First  Early: topic and question  Test the topic's fit with digital society, the feasibility
  taking shape  of the question, and your research plan.

 Interim  Mid-process: research done,  Review the evidence gathered, the emerging argument,
  drafting underway  and any change of direction and why.

 Viva voce  After the final version is  A short concluding interview: what you learned, what
  complete  challenged you, how you grew as a researcher.

T H E 5 0 0 - W O R D R E F L E C T I V E S TAT E M E N T
After the viva voce you write one reflective statement of up to 500 words on the RPF. It carries all four
Criterion E marks, and it is marked against two strands: evaluative and growth. The mistake that costs
most students is recounting: first I chose a topic, then I did a survey, then I wrote a draft. The examiner
has just read your essay; they know what you did. They are asking what it did to you.

The IB's own examples of what growth looks like are worth building your statement around:
experiences or insights that will shape your future thinking; explicit examples of how skills you learned
transfer to other contexts; and an explanation of how a change in perspective changed your decisions.
Anchor each claim in one specific moment from your RRS.

FINAL CHECKLISTS
Criterion A: framework
  The research question is on the title page, in the introduction, and referred to throughout.
  The methods are named, justified, and described fully enough to repeat.
  The essay follows academic conventions: introduction, argument-led body, conclusion, references.

  Every figure and table is labelled and mentioned in the text.

Criterion B: knowledge and understanding
  The question sits explicitly in digital society: concepts, content, and contexts all named and used.
  Terminology is accurate, consistent, and present in every section, never decoration.
  The knowledge is in your own words; quotations are short and purposeful.
  Around 25 sources are cited, spread through the essay rather than clustered at the start.

Criterion C: analysis and line of argument
  Every analytical claim is backed by evidence from your research.
  Nothing is purely descriptive beyond the minimum needed to set up analysis.
