# IA guidelines for the presentation video (Criteria C, D, E)

> Packaged from `claude/00_SYSTEM_MASTER_REFERENCE.md` §1, `sources/IA/IA_05_Checklist_Outline_Video_C_D_E.md` and `claude/VIDEO_IA_Demo_Pipeline.md` §1. If this file and an official IB document disagree, the official document wins.

## 1. The IA in brief (Digital Society, first assessment 2027)

24 marks, same at SL and HL (30% SL / 20% HL). Two components:

- **Inquiry Process Document (IPD):** Sections A + B, at most 1,500 words combined.
- **Recorded multimedia presentation:** at most **10:00**; assesses **C, D and E**. Content after 10:00 is not credited. No candidate name or number.

| Crit. | Where | Marks | What top work shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Inquiry focus | IPD | 3 | Targeted question, cited real-world example, explained 3C connections |
| B Sources | IPD | 6 | Exactly 3 sources, each analysed and its usefulness justified |
| **C Analysis & evaluation** | Video | 6 | Explained system → sustained, evidenced analysis and evaluation → clear answer to the question |
| **D Emerging trends & future developments** | Video | 6 | Separate, labelled, researched, future-facing discussion linked to the inquiry |
| **E Communication** | Video (throughout) | 3 | Coherent structure; visuals, audio and citations support understanding |

Keep all assessed content within 10 minutes. Use the research to build an argument about people and communities; **do not read Sections A and B aloud.**

## 2. Checklist (every line must be met by the script and the visuals)

### Criterion C: Analysis and evaluation

**1 Inquiry focus**
- [ ] State the exact inquiry question used in the IPD.
- [ ] Briefly introduce the actual example, digital system, affected group and setting.
- [ ] Make the issue clear without repeating all of Section A.

**2 How the digital system works** (the most frequent omission)
- [ ] Identify the specific system or application and the relevant inputs or data.
- [ ] Explain how the system processes those inputs and what output or action results.
- [ ] Explain who uses the output and who controls relevant settings or decisions.
- [ ] Use a labelled diagram, interface or demonstration to connect the mechanism to the impacts; cite the technical evidence.

**3 Analysis of impacts and implications**
- [ ] Organise around impacts or themes, not one section per source.
- [ ] For each main claim: cited evidence, and how the system produces an effect for identified people. Paragraph engine: claim → cited evidence → mechanism → people affected → qualification or counter-evidence → judgement.
- [ ] Compare a variety of stakeholder perspectives and explain why experiences or interests differ.
- [ ] Weigh contrasting findings, benefits, harms, trade-offs and conditions; say which evidence is most persuasive and why.
- [ ] Use source limitations to qualify claims, including uncertainty about causation or generalisation.
- [ ] Reach reasoned judgements throughout, then combine them into an answer; no descriptive pros-and-cons list.

**4 Answer to the inquiry question**
- [ ] A clear answer stating the extent or nature of the effect, supported by the strongest evidence.
- [ ] Weigh the main counter-evidence or limitation; keep the answer within the population and setting studied.
- [ ] Complete this conclusion within C, **before** D begins.

Red flags: reading B aloud, a pros-and-cons list, uncited claims, survey results with no method, ignoring people and communities.

### Criterion D: Emerging trends and future developments
A clearly labelled section after the C conclusion; about 2–2.5 minutes; worth the same as C. Develop further insight into the same inquiry, not a repeat of the conclusion.

**5 Identify a relevant development**
- [ ] A specific developing practice, technical change, trial, adoption pattern or other development.
- [ ] Show how it connects to the digital system, people and issue already investigated.
- [ ] Depth over a catalogue of unrelated technologies.

**6 Establish the research evidence**
- [ ] Cite credible evidence that the development exists or supports the forecast.
- [ ] Distinguish a measured result from a proposal, announcement, marketing claim or aspiration.
- [ ] Explain limits of the evidence (small pilot, different population, preprint status).

**7 Explain implications and uncertainty**
- [ ] How the development could change the earlier impacts, stakeholder relationships or answer.
- [ ] The mechanism behind that change, not "technology will improve everything".
- [ ] Barriers, conditions and alternative outcomes; conditional forecasts where the evidence requires.

**8 Closing insight**
- [ ] What the future-facing evidence adds to understanding the original inquiry.
- [ ] What evidence or conditions would strengthen or change the judgement.
- [ ] Inside the assessed time limit.

### Criterion E: Communication throughout

**Structure and multimedia**
- [ ] Clear thematic headings and transitions.
- [ ] Narration and visuals in sync; explain the relevant part of each chart, diagram, image or demo.
- [ ] Text, labels, units, citations and evidence readable in the exported recording.
- [ ] Intelligible audio at a controlled pace; no distracting visuals or sound (no music).
- [ ] Captions, if used, checked against the speech.

**Evidence and references**
- [ ] Author or organisation and date whenever research is shown or discussed (readable on screen or clearly spoken).
- [ ] Credit images, charts, clips and audio as well as factual claims.
- [ ] Additional research is allowed; the video is not limited to the three Section B sources.
- [ ] Every presentation source and media item appears in the IPD reference list.
- [ ] Surveys: who responded, recruitment, n, method, main limitation, denominators with percentages.
- [ ] Interviews: interviewee's role, date, method and perspective before relying on responses.

**Final export checks**
- [ ] Time the complete recording including pauses and closing material.
- [ ] Watch the whole export: audio, visuals, captions, citations, ending.
- [ ] C conclusion appears before a clearly labelled D section; neither rushed or cut off.
- [ ] TTS reading the student's own script is allowed; another person reading it is not.

## 3. Running order (illustrative, 9:40)
| Time | Content |
|---|---|
| 0:00–1:20 | Inquiry question, example and digital system |
| 1:20–6:25 | Impacts, contrasting perspectives, comparison and synthesis |
| 6:25–7:10 | **Answer to the inquiry question (end of C)** |
| 7:10–9:25 | **D: emerging trends and future developments** |
| 9:25–9:40 | Closing insight, small timing buffer |

## 4. House rules for the script
1. Follow the IPD's exact inquiry question.
2. Call the three main sources **Source A, Source B and Source C** (never 1/2/3). Every claim is attributed aloud: "**According to Source X, …**". Other sources are named in full ("According to Noy and Zhang, …").
3. Introduce illustrations with "**for example**".
4. Write numbers as words so the TTS says them correctly; `subs.py` turns them back into digits.
5. Teacher/student voice (see `voice_rules.md`): keep the writer's own connectives (So, But, Why…?, This links back…), no stock phrases; run the drift check.
6. Never invent numbers. Anything the IPD lacks (e.g. a D development with evidence) is researched and cited, and the user is told what was added.
7. Never reuse topics or content from the sample IA/EE files.

## 5. Integrity
AI may read the student's own script via TTS, help find leads or transcribe their speech. AI must not write the question or the assessed thinking. A demo video made with this skill is a **teaching model**: in a student's own submission the student writes the script and acknowledges any AI use (keep an AI-use log: tool, date, prompt, purpose, output, how it was checked).

## 6. Scene plan (13 scenes, about 1,480 words at about 2.6 words/s → about 9:38)

| Scene | Section label | Content |
|---|---|---|
| s01 | Introduction | Inquiry focus: exact question, example, system, people, setting |
| s02 | Introduction | How the digital system works: input → processing → output → human, who controls it, plus one "for example" (labelled diagram) |
| s03–s04 | Analysis | Theme 1, with a qualification |
| s05–s06 | Analysis | Theme 2, with the owner/other stakeholder perspective |
| s07 | Analysis | Theme 3 |
| s07b | Analysis | Weighing the evidence: strength / limit / weight per source (table) |
| s08 | Conclusion | Answer to the inquiry question, with its limitation (still Criterion C) |
| s09–s11 | Development | Three emerging trends / future developments (labelled D) |
| s12 | Conclusion | Closing insight: what would change the judgement, plus a values/equity link |

Padding: 13 × 1.15 s (LEAD 0.35 s + TAIL 0.8 s). Measure the chosen voice's pace on the first clip: a 2.5 words/s voice turns 1,491 words into 10:07, fixed with `atempo≈1.03` rather than cutting content. Target 9:30–9:55.
